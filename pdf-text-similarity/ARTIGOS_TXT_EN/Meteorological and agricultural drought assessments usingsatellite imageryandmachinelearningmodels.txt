Abstract
Drought is one of the most frequent and impactful climatic hazards in Iran, with signiﬁcant consequences for water resources, agri-
culture, and food security. This study aims to provide a comprehensive assessment of both meteorological and agricultural droughts and
to examine their interrelationship across Iran. A set of diverse drought indices, including Standardized Precipitation Index (SPI), Stan-
dardized Precipitation Evapotranspiration Index (SPEI), Vegetation Condition Index (VCI), Temperature Condition Index (TCI), Eva-
porative Drought Index (EDI), and Soil Moisture Deﬁcit Index (SMDI), was utilized to evaluate drought dynamics. Temporal analysis
of these indices revealed that meteorological drought events often occurred concurrently across the country, though their intensity varied
by region. Pearson correlation analysis between SPI and other indices showed a general increase in correlation with longer temporal
scales, highlighting the dampening eﬀect of short-term variability. Among the indices, SPEI showed the highest correlation with SPI,
reﬂecting the dominant inﬂuence of precipitation in both indices. Spatial analysis indicated that the strongest correlations between mete-
orological and agricultural drought indices were observed in the western, southwestern, and some northern regions of Iran, areas char-
acterized by natural vegetation cover and rangelands that are directly inﬂuenced by rainfall. Furthermore, the predictability of the each
agricultural drought index using climatic and other drought indices was evaluated using Sequential Feature Selection (SFS). Overall,
EDI and SPI emerged as the most inﬂuential predictors across all target indices, albeit with diﬀering dominance depending on the target
variable: EDI was most critical for vegetation (VCI) and soil moisture (SMDI), whereas SPI dominated for TCI and EDI estimation.
Results indicated that meteorological droughts occur concurrently across much of the country, though intensity varies regionally, with
the western and southwestern zones showing the strongest correlations between meteorological and agricultural drought. EDI and SPI
were identiﬁed as the most inﬂuential predictors across indices, whereas vegetation- and temperature-based indicators exhibited lagged
responses to precipitation deﬁcits. The framework achieved robust predictive accuracy, demonstrating the value of combining multiple
drought indices with machine learning to capture nonlinear interactions. By advancing beyond conventional composite index
approaches, this study provides actionable insights for drought early warning, agricultural planning, and water resource management
in drought-prone regions of Iran.

1. Intr oduction
Drought is a slow-onset, destructive hazard aﬀecting 
water, agriculture, ecosystems, and economies. Deﬁned as 
prolonged precipitation reduction below long-term aver-
ages, it occurs across all climates, from high to low rainfall
zones. Its gradual development complicates detection and
management, making close monitoring essential for timely
responses and eﬀective mitigation strategies (e.g., Mishra 
and Singh 2010; Alahacoon et al. 2021; Orimoloye 2022;
Mullapudi et al. 2023; Senapati and Das, 2024a and b
Senapati et al., 2025a and b). 
Numerous studies have been carried out at local and 
global scales to investigate drought characteristics, trends,
and associated impacts. For example, Spinoni et al.
(2014) employed the Standardized Precipitation Index 
(SPI) to assess drought characteristics on a global scale 
under changing climate conditions. In their study, SPI-12 
was calculated monthly using a 60-year baseline (1951– 
2010), based on precipitation data from the GPCC dataset. 
They produced global maps of drought frequency, dura-
tion, and severity for three time periods: 1951–1970, 
1971–1990, and 1991–2010. Their analysis revealed spatial
and temporal variations in drought hotspots, with a signif-
icant increase in drought components observed in regions
such as Africa, Eastern Asia, the Mediterranean, and
Southern Australia. In another study, Liu et al. (2021)
incorporated both precipitation and Potential Evapotran-
spiration (PET) to assess meteorological drought. They 
applied the SPI and the Standardized Precipitation Evapo-
transpiration Index (SPEI) to examine the spatial and tem-
poral 
characteristics 
of 
drought 
across 
diﬀerent 
geomorphological regions of Sichuan Province, China, 
using meteorological data from 44 stations spanning 1961 
to 2019. By employing the Mann–Kendall trend test and 
Multiple Linear Regression (MLR), they found that 
droug ht trends were more signiﬁcant in plains and hilly
areas compared to plateaus and mountainous regions.
Although elevation alone was not identiﬁed as the domi-
nant factor in precipitation variability, it was shown along-
side
temperature,
longitude,
and
latitude
to
jointly
inﬂuence spatial precipitation patterns.
Meteorological drought strongly impacts the environ-
ment, with agriculture being most vulnerable due to sensi-
tivity to reduced rainfall and higher evapotranspiration. 
This led to the c oncept of agricultural drought, deﬁned
by its eﬀects on soil moisture, crop growth, and productiv-
ity (Hooshyaripor et al., 2022). Various quantitative 
indices have been developed to assess agricultural drought, 
monitor trends, an d examine its link with meteorological
drought. For instance, Shahzaman et al. (2021) conducted 
a study across South Asia, including Afghanistan, Pak-
istan, India, and Bangladesh, and evaluated the perfor-
mance of several satellite-based drought indices, namely 
the Evaporative Stress Index (ESI), Vegetation Health
Index (VHI), Enhanced Vegetation Index (EVI), and Stan-
dardized Anomaly Index (SAI), over the period 2002–2019.
The study identiﬁed 2002 as a severe drought year, with 
particularly prolonged drought conditions in India 
(7 months), followed by Pakistan and Afghanistan 
(6 months), and Bangladesh (3 months). Spatial analysis 
revealed that regions such as southeast Pakistan, northern, 
northwestern, and southwestern India, and Afghanistan 
were heavily aﬀected. The ﬁndings high lighted soil mois-
ture as a critical variable in drought initiation, and con-
cluded
that
among
the
evaluated
indices,
ESI
demonstrated the highest sensitivity and timeliness, making
it a reliable tool for agricultural drought monitoring in the
region.

In Zhang et al.’s (2021) investigation, as a case study in

assessed using the Standardized Precipitation Index (SPI), 
the Standardized Soil Moisture Index (SSMI), and the 
Gravity Recovery and Climate Experiment (GRACE)-

the humid Yangtze River Basin and the arid/semi-arid Yel-

Cao et al. (2022) employed SPEI and the VCI to assess

Northwest China during 2000–2019. Their ﬁndings high-
lighted that both drought types showed a decreasing trend 
across various climatic zones and vegetation types, suggest-
ing improved moisture conditions in the region. The study 
further revealed that the response of agricultural drought 
to meteorological drought was strongest in semiarid 
e cosystems and varied depending on local environmental
sensitivity. Dai et al. (2022) performed a study on the 
Wei River Basin in China employing the SPI and Standard-
ized Soil Moisture Index (SSMI) to analyze the propaga-
tion of meteorological drought to agricultural drought. 
The researchers used conditional probability analysis 
across multiple time scales to determine the propagation 
time and identiﬁed seasonal patterns through a moving 
window approach and Mann-Kendall trend test. Their
ﬁndings revealed that the drought propagation time is


becoming shorter, especially in the main-stream basin. 
While temperature increases, a decrease in soil moisture 
and precipitation wer e identiﬁed as key drivers of this
propagation, irrigation water use had limited inﬂuence.

Using multiple statistical metrics and three drought indices

Overall, TRMM and PERSIANN-CDR outperformed
CHIRPS in detecting drought events. In a similar study,
Mianabadi et al. (2022) analyzed drought trends in south-
eastern Iran using the Mann–Kendall test and CHIRPS 
precipitation data during a 40-year period begining in 
1981. CHIRPS aligned well with ground data at monthly 
and annual scales but was weaker daily. Results showed 
shorter-duration droughts (3–9 months) became less fre-
quent yet more intense, while long-term droughts (12–
24 months) showed no clear trend. Overall, SPI trends indi-
cated no signiﬁcant long-term drying in the region over the
past four decades. Additionally, Shamloo et al. (2022)
examined agricultural drought trends in the Urmia Lake 
Basin from 2001 to 2021 using a set of remote–sensing–b 
ased vegetation and drought indices, including EVI, VCI, 
Temperature Condition Index (TCI), and Temperature

delayed vegetation response to rainfall. A notable negative
Correlation Coeﬃcient [r] of −0.73 between TCI and

introduced the Combined Drought Index (CDI), which 
integrates VCI, TCI, Soil Water Index (SWI), and Precip-
itation Condition Index (PCI) to provide a comprehensive 
measure of drought, covering both meteorological and 
agricultural aspects. The study focused on monitoring 
drought in the Karkheh Basin, southwestern Iran, from 
2001 to 2018. Using Principal Component Analysis 
(PCA), they developed the CDI index, which was shown
to correlate strongly with traditional drought indices like
SPI and SDI. The ﬁndings highlight the eﬀectiveness of
CDI in comprehensive drought monitoring. Between 1960
and 2019, Wang et al. (2022) examined meteorological

revealed risin g drought-aﬀected areas in spring (85.8 %),
summer (77.4 %), and autumn (97.7 %), but only 12.9 %
in winter, highlighting signiﬁcant seasonal and spatial vari-

(2022) assessed the impact of meteorological drought on

patterns. Results showed maize yields in south Punjab were 
highly sensitive to short-term droughts during critical
growth stages, with drought explaining ∼27 % of yield
variability. Increasing drought sensitivity was observed in

spatial diﬀerences in impacts.

have examined drought trends using either meteorological 
or agricultural indices, most have focused on a limited set

the multidimensional nature of drought. Moreover, the 
application of advanced machine learning techniques, such 
as Random Forest regression combined with feature selec-
tion, remains limited in regional drought research across 
Iran. This gap restricts our understanding of how diﬀerent 
drought indicators interact and propagate across diverse
climatic zones. Addressing this gap, the present study
develops
a
comprehensive
framework
that
integrates
diverse
meteorological
and
agricultural
indices
with
machine learning models to explore their interconnections
and predictive relationships at the national scale. This
approach advances drought monitoring in Iran by moving
beyond single-index assessments, oﬀering deeper insights

While earlier research in Iran, such as Karimi et al.
(2022), has proposed integrated indices like the Combined 
Drought Index (CDI) to merge meteorological and agricul-
tural indicators, these approaches primarily rely on statisti-
cal aggregation methods. In contrast, the present study 
introduces a machine learning–based framework that not 
only integrates diverse indices but also quantiﬁes their pre-
dictive relationships through Random Forest regression 
and Sequential Feature Selection. This methodological 
advancement allows experts to evaluate the relative impor-
tance of diﬀerent drought indica tors, uncover nonlinear
interactions, and improve the accuracy of agricultural
drought prediction across varied climatic zones. Therefore,
the novelty of this study lies in moving beyond static com-
posite indices toward a dynamic, data-driven integration of
meteorological and agricultural drought indices using
advanced machine learning models.
Despite the availability of various drought indices, lim-
ited studies have investigated the quantitative relationship 
between climatic and agricultural droughts using integrated 
modeling approaches. Several studies have been conducted 
in the ﬁeld of drought monitoring, but there is still a need 
for a more accurate and comprehensive understanding of 
the relationship between climatic and agricultural drought. 
Many previous studies have focused on one or two indica-
tors, and have rarely analyzed simultaneously diﬀerent 
types of drought, such as vegetation and surface stresses. 
However, the simultaneous use of various meteorological 
and agricultural indicators such as SPI, SPEI, Evaporative 
Drought Index (EvDI), TCI, VCI, and Soil Moisture Def-
icit Index (SMDI) can provide a multidimensional view of
climatic conditions and vegetation cover. Evaluating the


relationships between these indicators allows for the identi-
ﬁcation of correlations, recognition of their spatial and 
temporal diﬀerences, and understanding of the process of 
drought transition from meteorological drought to agricul-
tural drought. This process can greatly help identify vulner-
able locations and manage resources. The relationship 
between meteorological indices such as SPI, SPEI, and
EvDI with agricultural indices such as TCI, VCI, and
SMDI makes it possible to examine how climatic condi-
tions such as decreased precipitation or increased tempera-
ture aﬀect the response of vegetation.

This research aims to conduct a comprehensive evalua-
tion of drought across the entire country of Iran, with a 
particular focus on understanding the relationship and 
impact of climatic drought on agricultural drought in dif-
ferent climatic zones of the country. The study will employ 
both quantitative and qualitative analysis to assess the 
extent and nature of this interaction, as well as to calibrate 
the results. In this regard, the research will utilize the most 
important and recent drought indices, including SPI, SPEI, 
VCI, TCI, SMDI, and EDI, derived from the latest remote 
sensing data, to facilitate spatial and temporal analysis of 
drought events. By doing so, the study intends to provide 
a more accurate and nuanced understanding of drought 
patterns and their eﬀects across varying climates in Iran. 
Also, this study aims to investigate the relationship 
between climatic and agricultural droughts by applying 
correlation analysis and regression models to better under-
stand the nature of their interaction. In this context Ran-
dom Forest regression model is used to explore the
predictability of agricultural drought indices. Modeling
and estimating these indices based on climatic drought
indices provides a practical approach for assessing the link-
age between climatic and agricultural drought. Further-
more, clustering techniques are utilized to detect and
interpret spatial patterns of similar drought occurrences
across Iran, allowing for targeted analysis and decision-
making. It helps uncover hidden patterns in drought
dynamics, facilitating more eﬀective drought monitoring
and management strategies.

2. Study domain
The study area encompasses Iran, located in the Middle 
East, characterized by diverse physical features, climate,
and
topography
(Fig. 1). It covers an area of 
1,648,195 km2, stretching from 25°4′33″ N to 39°46′45″ 
N in latitude and from 44°2′2.5″ E to 66°19′49″ E in longi-
tude. Iran exhibits a diverse climate, ranging from arid con-
ditions to temperate, no dry-season climates along the
Caspian coast and within the northern forests. The coun-
try’s topography is highly complex, dominated by two
major mountain ranges: the Alborz Mountains, stretching
east to west, and the Zagros Mountains, extending from 
the northwest to the southeast. Meteorological drought, 
characterized by an extended duration of below-average 
precipitation, plays a crucial role in triggering and acceler-
ating desertiﬁcation processes. This type of drought leads 
to a signiﬁcant reduction in soil moisture and vegetation
cover, which in turn increases the vulnerability of land to
degradation, particularly in arid and semi-arid regions
(Emadodin et al. 2019).

the fundamental pillars of Iran’s economy, and it is recog-

accounts for approximately 9 % of the country’s Gross

of national employment (Maghrebi et al., 2020. Therefore,

country’s overall economic growth. In many developing

of the main priorities in agricultural development programs

tural drought in Iran poses a signiﬁcant challenge, impact-

Also, all MODIS products are publicly available from
the Earth Data website.

The SPI is a widely used meteorological drought index 
that quantiﬁes precipitation anomalies over multiple time 
scales. It is based solely on precipitation data, making it 
versatile and applicable in regi ons with limited climatic
records (Vicente-Serrano and Lo´pez-Moreno 2 005). The 
SPI can be computed by ﬁtting a long-term precipitation

gamma distribution (Liu et al. 2 021), which formulations 
of SPI were deeply provided in Supplementary Materia l.


(Livada and Assimakopoulos 2007). The resulting SPI val-
ues indicate the number of standard deviations by which 
the observed precipitation deviates from the climatological 
mean. Positive SPI values denote wetter-than-normal con-
ditions, while negative values indicate drought.

The SPEI is an advanced drought index that integrates 
precipitation and Potential Evapotranspiration (PET) to 
assess climatic water balance anomalies over time. SPEI

SPEI values (Liu et al. 2021). Formulations of SPEI was 
fully described in Supplementary Mater ial. In this study,

4.4. Agro-climatic drought connection
As previously stated, climatic drought inﬂuences agri-
cultural drought, either directly or indirectly. In other 
words, agricultural drought is largely driven by the occur-
rence and intensity of climatic drough t. In this study, two
main approaches are employed to analyze the relationship
between these two types of droughts.
4.4.1. Linear r elationship
In the ﬁrst stage of the analysis, the focus is on quanti-
fying the linear relationship between climatic drought and 
agricultural drought. To investigate the relationship 
between climatic and agricultural droughts, correlation 
analysis is employed as a straightforward and eﬀective sta-
tistical method. This approach allows for quantifying the 
strength and direction of association between t he two
drought types across diﬀerent regions and time scales
(Huang et al. 2015;Behrang Manesh et al. 2019;S un et al.
2023). To achieve this objective, Pearson correlation anal-
ysis was conducted between the climatic drought indices 
(SPI and SPEI) and the agricultural drought indices (i.e., 
VCI, TCI, SMDI, and EDI) across all 607 sub-basins.

6-, and 12-month. The use of multiple time scales aims to
capture the temporal dynamics of drought impacts and

multi-scale approach provides deeper insi ght into the
lagged and scale-dependent relationships between the two

4.4.2. Predictive capacity
In the second approach, the goal is to assess the extent 
to which agricultural and climatic drought indices are 
interrelated by evaluating the predictive capacity of cli-
matic drought indicators in estimating the agricultural 
drought indices. In addition to linear correlation analysis, 
regression modeling is employed to explore deeper and 
potentially nonlinear relationships between climatic and 
agricultural droughts. While correlation measures the 
strength of linear associations, it is often insuﬃcient to cap-
ture the full complexity of drought interactions. Regression
analysis allows for modeling more intricate relationships,
identifying patterns and inﬂuences that may not be evident
through simple linear metrics (Tian et al. 2018).

variables no longer yields a signiﬁcant improvement in
accuracy. Based on this process, each predictor is assigned
an importance rank according to its inﬂuence on model

6. Comparisons with related works
Drought signiﬁcantly aﬀects various aspects of human 
life, ecosyste ms, and the environment. Iran, situated
predominantly in arid and semi-arid climate zones, has 
experienced, and will likely continue to experience, serious 
consequences due to drought. Among its various dimen-
sions, drought is commonly discussed in both climatic 
and agricultural contexts. These two types are closely inter-
related, as climatic factors often act as primary drivers 
inﬂuencing plant health and agricultural productivity. In
this study, we explored the relationship between climatic
and agricultural droughts across Iran, aiming to provide
a
better
understanding
of
their
interactions
and
implications.

Iran’s climatic pattern and climatic zones are strongly 
inﬂuenced by two major mountain ranges, the Zagros 
and Alborz, as well as the presence of the northern and 
southern seas. Consequently, it is expected that these geo-
graphical features also contribute to shaping the country’s 
drought regime. The clustering results o f the 20-year pat-
terns of climatic droughts (as shown in Fig. 4) partially 
reﬂect the impact of these climatic and topographic pat-
terns on the spatial distribution of drought. For instance, 
the variation in clusters from the northwest to the south 
and southwest of Iran, observed in both SPEI and SPI 
indices, aligns with the orientation of the Zagros Moun-
tains, as these highlands act as a natural barrier that inﬂu-
ences precipitation patterns by inducing orographi c rainfall
and modulating the penetration of moisture-laden air
masses (Livada and Assimakopoulos 2007;Sabzipar var
et al. 20 15). Moreover, the existence of a distinct cluster 
(C1) in the southern regions adjacent to open waters may


reﬂect the inﬂuence of dominant regional climate systems. 
This area is signiﬁcantly aﬀected by moisture ﬂows from 
the Persian Gulf and the Gulf of Oma n, the summer mon-
soon systems (particularly in the southeast), and Mediter-
ranean systems during the cold seasons (Saligheh and 
Sayadi 2017;Jamshidi and Samani 2022). Finally, Cluster 
C4 is mainly located in the eastern (SPI clusters) and 
east-central (SPEI clusters) regions of the country, which 
are generally known as arid zones. These areas are aﬀec ted
by the natural barriers of the Zagros and Alborz mountain
ranges, which block moist air masses from reaching the
interior regions.

The relationship between agricultural and climatic 
droughts is not limited to their temporal patterns; rather, 
examining this relationship in both temporal and spatial 
dimensions provides valuable insights (Behrang Manes h
et al. 20 19). Correlation analysis between climatic and agri-

various studies (Huang et al. 2015; Yang et al. 2023). In 
this research, we also assessed the relationship by calculat-
ing the Pearson correlation coeﬃcient between the SPI 
index and other drought indices. According t o previous
studies, such as Karimi et al. (20 22), drought indices may 
not always exhibit synchronous behavior, and the impact 
of climatic drought indices may appear with a temporal 
lag in agricultural drought responses. In this study as well, 
it was observed that increasing the temporal scale (Fig. 6), 
thereby reducing the inﬂuence of short-term ﬂuctuations, 
led to higher correlations between all indices and the SPI 
index. Among the indices, SPEI showed the highest corre-

spatial distribution of the correlation between the VCI
drought index, which is directly derived from vegetation


conditions, and the SPI index reveals that the highest cor-
relations are concentrated along the Zagros Mountains 
(western and southwestern regions) and, to some extent, 
the Alborz range. This pattern suggests that the presence 
of natural vegetation in the rangelands of these areas, 
which is highly dependent on precipitation, is a key factor 
contributing to the signiﬁcant relationship between the 
VCI and SPI drought indice s. In contrast, the lowest corre-
lations are observed in central and eastern parts of the
country, which encompass deserts and arid regions. The
lack of vegetation cover in these areas provides a reason-
able explanation for the weak correlations. This ﬁnding is
also supported by the study of Karimi et al. (2022), where 
sparse vegetation was identiﬁed as a limiting factor in 
establishing a strong relationship between climatic and 
agricultural droughts. The TCI index showed stronger spa-

directly impacts the TCI values. The SMDI (derived from

the western regions of the country (Fig. 6), indicating a 
strong dependency of soil moisture in these areas on pre-

Random Forest (RF) algorithm was applied to evaluate 
the predictive relationships between drought indices. RF 
is particularly suitable for this application due to its ability 
to handle nonlinear relationships, capture complex interac-

importance through ensemble decision trees. This is espe-

2021). Conversely, SPI was the leading predictor for TCI 
and EDI, underscoring the inﬂuence of precipitation-

the complementary nature of meteorological drought indi-
cators (SPI) and evaporative stress metrics (EDI and 
SMDI) in characterizing vegetation and soil moisture
responses. The RF approach not only identiﬁed key predic-

drought indices can enhance monitoring and improve early

under very low categories, validated through yield anomaly
index analysis. Senapati and Das (2024b) successfully 
delineated seven distinct alternative agriculture zones in 
the drought-prone UDRB using Remote Sensing (RS) 
and AHP-based Geographical Information System (GIS) 
techniques. The model’s accuracy was validated using 350 
ﬁeld sites, resulting in a high kappa coeﬃcient of 95.34 %
and an overall accuracy of 96 %.

In Senapati et al.’s (2025a) research, the frequency of 
drought events over the 36-year period was estimated at 
10–12 occurrences under both scenarios, with drought 
intensity ranging between –1.15 and –1.20. The central part 
of the UDRB was found to be more sensitive to drought 
frequency. Qualitatively, their study emphasized the utility 
of SPI for multi-scale drought assessment and highlights 
the importance of bias correction using the Empirical
Quantile Mapping (EQM) to improve model reliability.
The authors noted that SSP370 shows marginally better
performance in bias correction (correlation coeﬃcient of
0.91 vs. 0.89 for SSP585), which they attribute to its closer
alignment with India’s current socioeconomic trajectory.
Senapati et al. (2025b) used a mixed-methods approach, 
combining remote sensing with 385 farmer surveys. This 
revealed critical gaps, such as 68 % of farmers being unable 
to detect drought early, leading to actionable, community-
level policy recommendations like building farm ponds. In
contrast, the other study was a top-down, technically
advanced framework focused solely on physical system
dynamics, ideal for national-scale forecasting.
Senapati and Das (2025) developed the IADI using 
AHP to combine multiple remote sensing parameters, cre-
ating a more stable and accurate tool for real-time drought 
monitoring in rain-fed regions. Their study focused on 
improving assessment accuracy with historical data. In 
contrast, the present research likely emphasizes future cli-
mate risk, utilizing CMIP6 scenarios and standardized 
indices like SPI to project drought frequency and severity.
This forward-looking approach contributed more directly
to long-term adaptation planning, whereas the IADI
framework is optimized for current operational monitoring
and resource allocation.
7. Conc lusions
This study has provided a comprehensive, national-scale 
assessment of the interrelationships between meteorologi-
cal and agricultural droughts across Iran by integrating 
multiple satellite-derived indices and a machine learning 
framework over a 20-year period (2004–2023). The 
research 
successfully 
moved 
beyond 
single-index 
approaches to deliver a nuanced understanding of drought 
dynamics, revealing critical spatial and temporal patterns. 
The clustering of long-term drought indices delineated Iran 
into six distinct regions, with patterns strongly inﬂuenced 
by major topographic features like the Zagros and Alborz 
mountain ranges. While major drought events, such as 
those in 2008 and 2021, occurred synchronously across
the country, the clustering analysis revealed signiﬁcant
regional variations in intensity. The correlation analysis
demonstrated a clear scale-dependence in the relationship
between meteorological and agricultural droughts. For
instance, the correlation between SPI and SPEI strength-
ened markedly from an average of approximately 0.17 at
the 3-month scale to 0.81 at the 12-month scale, highlight-
ing how longer temporal scales dampen short-term vari-
ability and be tter capture prolonged water deﬁcits.
569
Spatially, the linkages between drought types exhibited 
distinct regional sensitivities. The strongest correlations 
between SPI and agricultural indices like VCI and SMDI 
were observed in western and southwestern Iran, where 
correlation coeﬃcients often exceeded 0.4 at the 12-
month scale. These areas, characterized by natural vegeta-
tion, showed a direct dependence on precipitation. In con-
trast, the central and eastern arid deserts consistently 
showed the weakest correlations, frequently below 0.2, 
due to sparse vegetation cover and limited soil moisture 
retention. The application of Random Forest regression 
with Sequential Feature Selection quantiﬁed the predictive 
drivers of agricultural drought, revealing that the EDI and
the SPI were the most inﬂuential predictors. EDI was the
dominant feature for predicting VCI and SMDI, while
SPI was most critical for estimating TCI. The modeling
framework itself demonstrated robust performance, with
the estimation of EDI achieving the highest test accuracy
(R2 values between 0.72 and 0.82 across clusters). In con-
trast, SMDI proved more challenging to predict, yielding
higher RMSE values up to 1.20, indicating the complex
nature of soil moisture dynamics.
Beyond aligning with previous studies, our ﬁndings 
carry important implications for drought management 
and policymaking in Iran. The identiﬁcation of SPI and 
EDI as dominant predictors underscores the need to prior-
itize precipitation and evapotranspiration monitoring 
within national drought early warning systems. Incorporat-
ing satellite-based indices into operational frameworks 
could improve real-time tracking of agricultural stress, 
especially in western and southwestern Iran where 
drought–vegetation linkages are strongest. Furthermore, 
the machine learning framework demonstrated here pro-
vides a scalable tool for integrating diverse indicators into 
decision-support systems, enabling policymakers to antici-
pate agricultural drought impacts more accurately and 
allocate water resources more eﬃciently. At the regional
level, the observed lag between meteorological and agricul-
tural drought signals suggests that proactive irrigation
scheduling, crop insurance schemes, and adaptive planting
calendars could buﬀer farmers against yield losses. Overall,
embedding such data-driven insights into Iran’s drought
management strategies would enhance resilience in the
agricultural sector and support long-term food and water
security under climate variability.
In conclusion, this study advances drought monitoring 
in Iran by establishing a dynamic, data-driven framework 
that eﬀectively captures the propagation of meteorological 
deﬁcits into agricultural impacts. The ﬁndings underscore 
the necessity of adopting multi-index, scale-aware, and 
region-speciﬁc strategies for drought early warning. The 
identiﬁed spatial patterns, such as the high vulnerability 
of western agricultural zones and the predictive dominance
of EDI and SPI, oﬀer actionable insights for policymakers


to prioritize interventions and enhance resilience to 
drought in a predominantly arid and vulnerable country.
