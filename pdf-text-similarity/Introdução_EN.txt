INTRODUCTION

Tourism is a strategic driver of socioeconomic development and income decentralization in many developing countries, particularly in regions affected by the contraction of traditional industrial and agricultural activities. In this context, alternative forms of tourism—most notably rural tourism, ecotourism, agritourism, and community-based tourism—emerge as viable pathways to diversify local productive structures. These activities foster the valorization of local culture, enable families to remain in rural areas, promote social inclusion, and strengthen community pride. Unlike mass tourism, these modalities emphasize local know‑how, the authenticity of landscapes, and intangible cultural heritage, generating livelihoods that complement family farming without eroding rural identity.

However, the mere existence of natural or cultural attractions does not guarantee regional development (Caetano, Wegner & Xavier, 2023). The economic base of many communities remains fragile due to insufficient public planning and scarce investment. Such resources are essential to support a sector that, in rural areas alone, has the potential to affect approximately 25 million Brazilians (IBGE, 2022).

The expansion of rural tourism in Brazil unfolds amid pronounced structural heterogeneity: modern and highly productive sectors coexist with pockets of subsistence. This disparity is reflected in the spatial distribution of funding and credit: while regional hubs and state capitals concentrate most private investment and large‑scale infrastructure, small localities oriented toward community ecotourism face acute liquidity constraints.

In the absence of integrated policies and adequate investment, the operational success of a destination depends on organizational articulation and the territory’s capacity to convert assets into marketable and sustainable products. This conversion is crucial for tourism diversification to generate cultural value, preserve biodiversity, and foster local socioeconomic progress. Limited access to formal finance and insufficient support infrastructure (e.g., telecommunications and transport) remain the principal bottlenecks to sustainable tourism development in non‑urban areas (UN Tourism, 2023).

This study’s central objective is to analyze municipal structures for the development of community‑based ecotourism by assessing how the circulation of financial capital in rural areas interacts with productive supply and destination attractiveness. The selection of Bahia and Rio Grande do Sul is justified by their contrasting geographic and institutional profiles: Rio Grande do Sul has an established history of rural routes and integrated credit cooperativism, whereas Bahia concentrates large‑scale tourist poles along the coast and faces the challenge of internalizing tourism revenues toward the semi‑arid interior and the Chapada Diamantina.

Accordingly, this research proposes the formulation of a Synthetic Territorial Viability Index (Índice Sintético de Viabilidade Territorial, ISVT) as a geointelligence tool for tourism governance in Bahia (BA) and Rio Grande do Sul (RS). The ISVT aims to map latent contrasts and provide evidence to inform integrated public‑policy planning for regional development and targeted support.
